Tyre manufacturers keep their rubber compound recipes secret, and smaller producers
recover them by reverse engineering finished tyres. We want to know whether a recipe
can instead be designed computationally from published methods and open data.

Research question: can published machine-learning methods for polymers and rubber,
together with open data, predict the properties of a tyre rubber compound from its
recipe well enough to propose new recipes with target properties?

Data: the attached archive holds tires_2.csv, 3774 compounds collected from 337
patents. Columns: recipe in phr (elastomers, fillers, oil, cure system), grades and
types of ingredients, process parameters, and measured properties with gaps (Mooney
viscosity, M100, M300, tensile strength, elongation at break, hardness, tan delta at
0 and 60 C, abrasion, cure times). Rows from one patent are near copies of each other.

Published methods to examine. Find which of them have code or models that actually
run, and use the ones that do:
- polyBERT, Kuenneth and Ramprasad, Nature Communications 2023,
  doi:10.1038/s41467-023-39868-6, model copy: https://huggingface.co/xushijie/polyBERT
- PolymerGNN, Queen et al., npj Computational Materials 2023,
  doi:10.1038/s41524-023-01034-3, https://github.com/owencqueen/PolymerGNN
- TransPolymer, Xu et al., npj Computational Materials 2023,
  https://github.com/ChangwenXu98/TransPolymer
- Wan et al., Composites Communications 2024, doi:10.1016/j.coco.2024.102072
- Wan et al., Chinese Journal of Polymer Science 2024, doi:10.1007/s10118-024-3216-3
- Hu et al., Journal of Materials Informatics 2024, doi:10.20517/jmi.2024.11
- Roy Choudhury et al. 2025, and the soft-sensor papers on Mooney viscosity,
  doi:10.3390/polym14051018 and doi:10.3390/s20030695
Search the literature for further methods with public code and add the ones you find.

What we need:
1. A short survey: for each method, what it predicts, how it encodes a compound,
   and whether its code, weights, and data are available and run.
2. Forward models for Mooney viscosity and M300, and for other properties if the data
   allows. Compare the plain recipe representation with representations obtained from
   the published methods that run. Report R2 and MAE for a random split and for a split
   where whole patents are held out, and say which of the two reflects use on a new recipe.
3. An inverse step: for a passenger tread compound with Mooney viscosity 55 to 65 and
   M300 of at least 10 MPa, propose five candidate recipes, with the predicted
   properties and an honest statement of how far these predictions can be trusted.
4. A report: what works, what does not run and why, which hypotheses were supported
   or rejected, and what a follow-up study should start from.

A negative result is acceptable if it is documented. Keep every script and intermediate
file that a follow-up study could reuse, and leave the published methods that run
available as callable tools, so that the next study does not have to set them up again.
